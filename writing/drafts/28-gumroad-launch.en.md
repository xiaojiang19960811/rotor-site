# Gumroad Launch Notes: Three Digital Products

> Column: Build Notes ｜ Collection: Solo Building ｜ Source: memory/2026-10-01.md (Gumroad launch log) ｜ Status: first draft

Three prompt-pack digital products launched on Gumroad: a $9 50-prompt starter pack, a $29 308-prompt full pack, and a $39 bundle of "308 prompts + a 19-page English ebook." Looking back, the most valuable part of the launch wasn't "it's live" — it was several assumptions disproved by actual testing.

## Pricing: three tiers, three jobs

$9 is the entry tier: 50 curated prompts, a low enough bar for a first purchase. $29 is the full tier: all 308 prompts, selling "never search again." $39 is the bundle tier: the full pack plus a 19-page English ebook, *The AI Productivity Playbook*, selling the system.

There's no single "best value" answer across the three — they map to three buying psychologies: let me try, give me everything, give me the method. Pricing gets set first and adjusted after validation.

## Before launch: QA and copy compliance

Two checks ran before launch. First, content QA: all 308 prompts verified English-only, sequentially numbered, no Chinese remnants, each with fillable placeholders, body averaging about 271 characters; 5 spot-checked, concrete structure, executable. Positioning converged on "a time-saving utility toolbox," not "exclusive secrets" — the former needs QA, the latter needs evidence.

Second, copy compliance cleanup: removed unverified superlatives like "battle-tested" and "tested and refined," removed the unfulfilled "$9 credit toward upgrade" promise. Only after a command-line recheck came back clean did anything ship. Selling digital products, every adjective in the copy is a future refund reason.

## An assumption disproved by testing: "sell first, connect payout later"

Before launch there was a taken-for-granted assumption: list first, start selling, leave money in the platform balance, connect payout later. Clicking publish for real, Gumroad required at least one payout method connected — no skip option in settings. The assumption died on contact with reality, and all three products stayed as drafts.

First lesson of cross-border indie business: platform rules are whatever testing shows, not whatever you've heard.

## The China-seller payout trap

Gumroad has no bank-payout channel for mainland China sellers. The verified conclusion: only a real-name PayPal China account works. After the user connected PayPal under Settings → Payouts and confirmed the connection, the three products were published one by one. Publishing meant only clicking Publish — no price, copy, or file changes. Everything decidable was decided before launch; nothing improvised at the moment of going live.

## Covers: WEBP rejected

A small trap: Gumroad rejects WEBP covers, accepting only JPEG/PNG/GIF. All three covers were converted to PNG and re-uploaded, previews fine. Traps like this aren't worth a second postmortem — just write them down.

## Status: launched, unverified

All three products are formally published, public pages accessible, prices and buy buttons displaying correctly. As of this writing there are no sales — that's a fact, not a failure. The market-validation window is about a week post-launch, watching real visits and conversions, not making wishes on launch day.

## In short

The launch checklist is short: content QA passed, no exaggerated copy, payout connected, cover formats compliant. Every line was earned by hitting a real blocker. A digital product doesn't sell files — it sells the certainty of "opens and works, description is true."
